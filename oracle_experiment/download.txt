1) Does the paper contribute to the body of knowledge?
Yes, moderately. The paper is a careful held-out evaluation of 14 frozen
breadth/token-cap policies for Qwen3-0.6B on GSM8K and MATH-500, with
realized-token accounting.

It reports three findings:
  - a clear negative result for a prompt-length joint heuristic on MATH-500;
  - measured token savings of 20–21% for an ESC-style two-sample stopping
    rule, now correctly presented as accuracy-identical by construction;
  - a post-hoc force-answer control showing that much of the low-cap deficit
    is caused by hard truncation.

The contribution is empirical and methodological rather than a new method.

2) Is the paper technically sound?
Yes. I recomputed all of the following, and all are correct:
  - all Wilson intervals;
  - all exact McNemar p-values and Holm adjustments in Tables 3 and 5;
  - the Test McNemar results in Sects. 7.1–7.2, consistent with the declared
    13-test Holm family;
  - the token savings in Tables 2–3;
  - the response-category counts in Sect. 7.3.

One minor inconsistency: the GSM8K L = 512 parse-failure rate is reported as
58.2%, while the category counts imply 59.25% unless some
truncated-after-thinking outputs were parseable.

The structural equality of breadth stopping and fixed k = 4 is now proved and
stated. Remaining limitations, all acknowledged:
  - a single generation seed;
  - stop-at-k = 2 rates reported for only one of six breadth-stopping
    configurations;
  - an unstated effective top-k value.

3) Is the subject matter presented in a comprehensive manner?
Mostly. The revision adds the following:
  - truncation and parse-failure rates;
  - the Holm families;
  - discordant counts and paired bootstrap intervals;
  - the knapsack frontier algorithm;
  - the MATH normalization;
  - the excluded item;
  - explicit thresholds with an algorithm box.

Still missing:
  - stop rates for MATH and the other caps;
  - Train escalation-event counts;
  - the relation between the historical 100-item manifest and the 0.6B
    GSM8K Train/Validation split.

The force-answer result is not yet integrated into the Discussion, which
still attributes low-cap failures to insufficient caps.

4) Are the references provided applicable and sufficient?
Yes. All 10 arXiv IDs and the DOIs resolve to the cited works. The page ranges
of [5] (Crossref: 6904–6917) and [8] (14302–14316) are still incorrect.

5) Are there references that are not appropriate? [ ] Yes  [X] No
5a) None.
